What every file is checked against

Most print problems come from file setup, not artwork. These are the defaults I work to, adjusted to your manufacturer's own requirements.

SettingStandardWhy it matters
ColorCMYK, printer's profileScreens show RGB. Converting early avoids colors going dull at print.
Resolution300 dpi at final sizeKeeps art sharp. Fine line art goes to 600 dpi.
Bleed3 mm each edgeCutting drifts slightly, so art runs past the cut line.
Safe zone3 mm inside the cutText, icons, and numbers never get trimmed off.
Text size7 pt rules, 6 pt minimumReadable at table distance, not just on screen.
BlackRich black for large areas, pure black for textSmall text in rich black blurs if plates shift.
FinishesFoil and spot UV on separate layersPrinters need them as their own plates.
FilesPDF/X, fonts embeddedWhat the printer opens is exactly what you approved.

Playability checks

Color plus shapeEvery card type has its own icon shape, so it still reads for colorblind players.
Symmetric backsCard backs look the same upside down, so no one can tell how a card was held.
Real-size test printsLayouts are printed at actual size and checked at arm's length before the full deck.
Sleeve fitCard size matches common sleeves, so players can protect the deck.

About three weeks, plus a printed proof

Week 3

Packaging, dieline, print-ready files, and store or campaign mockups.

Files go to your manufacturer for a physical proof.
